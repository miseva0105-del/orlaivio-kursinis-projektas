2. Select a design case and requirements

How to read the constraint diagram

Each point represents a combination of wing loading and power. At a fixed mass, moving to the right reduces wing area because S = W/(W/S). Each curve gives the minimum power needed to meet one requirement at a given wing loading. The feasible region lies above all power curves and to the left of the maximum wing loading. Compare feasible design points by wing area, power and margin.

How to obtain and justify the inputs

CD0 is the zero-lift drag coefficient in this simplified polar. The Cessna 162 value of 0.0333 is an assumption from Gudmundsson’s example. For your design, use a drag polar for a comparable configuration or sum the component contributions: CD0 ≈ Σ(Cf·FF·Q·Swet/S) + ΔCD0. Here Cf is the skin-friction coefficient, FF the form factor, Q the interference factor, Swet the wetted area and ΔCD0 the additional drag contributions. For an amphibious aircraft, evaluate the effect of floats, stepped hull and landing gear as applicable to the chosen configuration. Compare the baseline estimate with two alternatives. Calculation method and equations

1. General quantities

W = mg, X = W/S, q = ρV²/2, K = 1/(πeAR). Here m is design mass (kg), g = 9.80665 m/s², W is weight (N), S is wing area (m²), X is wing loading (N/m²), ρ is air density for the flight condition (kg/m³), V is true airspeed (m/s), q is dynamic pressure (Pa), AR is wing aspect ratio and e is the Oswald efficiency factor. For wingspan b, AR = b²/S. Reference aircraft wing loading is Xa = mag/Sa.

The model assumes a parabolic drag polar CD = CD0 + KCL². At load factor n, CL ≈ nX/q, so D/W ≈ qCD0/X + Kn²X/q. D is drag. The model uses the same W for all conditions.

3. From thrust to power

Thrust power is PT = TV, where T = (T/W)W. Propeller shaft power is Pprop = TV/ηp, where ηp is propeller efficiency at the operating point. Mechanical motor or engine output is Pmech = Pprop/ηdrive, where ηdrive is drivetrain efficiency. With direct drive and negligible coupling losses, these mechanical power values coincide.

An electric motor delivers mechanical power through its shaft. Motor electrical input power is Pel = Pmech/ηmotor. At the inverter’s DC input, PDC = Pel/ηinv. Estimating battery energy also requires mission phase durations, other electrical loads and the relevant losses.

Worked example. With T = 900 N, V = 50 m/s and ηp = 0.75, PT = 45 kW and Pprop = 60 kW. With direct drive the motor delivers 60 kW mechanically. At ηmotor = 0.94, the motor requires 63.8 kW of electrical input. At ηinv = 0.97, the inverter DC input is 65.8 kW. The efficiencies are teaching assumptions.

Diagram reference. The demonstrator defines αi = Pmech,available,i/Pmech,available,0. For condition i, P0,i = (T/W)iWVi/(1000ηp,iηdriveαi) in kW. The model uses one drivetrain efficiency for all conditions. For the Cessna 162 piston-engine case, α = 1.132(ρ/ρ₀) − 0.132, with ρ₀ = 1.225 kg/m³. For an electric powerplant, determine α from motor, inverter and cooling limits at the operating condition. A preliminary assumption of α = 1 means the available mechanical power equals the reference value.

At V = 0, TV is zero. Use static propeller performance data to determine static thrust and shaft power. The ground-roll power approximation uses ηp at liftoff speed.

4. Wing-loading limits and design point

Straight-flight stall. Xmax,S = ρSVS,max²CL,max/2. Turning stall. Xmax,turn = qCL,max,turn/n. The direct landing limit uses the entered Xmax,land. The combined limit is Xmax = min(Xmax,S, Xmax,turn, Xmax,land) over the active limits.

Power envelope. P0,req(X) = maxi P0,i(X). The T/W chart separately uses maxi(T/W)i. A point meets the active constraints when X ≤ Xmax and P0,sel ≥ P0,req(X). Wing area is S = W/X. Power margin is ΔP = P0,sel − P0,req. The sensitivity calculation evaluates 0.9X, X and 1.1X.

For a reference aircraft with mechanical engine or motor output stated on the same basis as the diagram, Pa,scaled = Pam/ma. This preserves its power-to-weight ratio while expressing power at the design aircraft mass.

3. Constraint diagram and design point

The selected power must meet the highest power requirement at the chosen wing loading. Vertical limits set the maximum permissible W/S. Use the table to identify the governing flight condition.

4. Benchmark and references

Cessna 162 benchmark at 10 lbf/ft²

The Cessna 162 case follows the Purdue AAE 451 recalculation based on Gudmundsson’s second edition (2022). Example 3–1 in the 2014 edition (pp. 64–65) uses a different aircraft with a design weight of 2000 lbf. At W/S = 10 lbf/ft² ≈ 478.8 N/m², the approximate benchmark T/W values are 0.187 (ground roll), 0.214 (climb), 0.125 (turn), 0.119 (cruise) and 0.094 (service ceiling). The landing limit is approximately 544.4 N/m². Dynamic pressures and speeds were converted to SI. The turn governs power and climb governs T/W at this point.
