1. Your individual design brief

Starting point

First understand what the brief asks you to achieve before developing a solution.

How do you distinguish a customer need from a design solution?

“Operate from small airfields” is a need that must be translated into verifiable performance criteria. “Use a high-wing configuration” is usually a possible design solution, unless the brief explicitly makes it a mandatory constraint.

2. Identify needs and constraints

Interpreting the brief

First identify the function, operating condition or outcome that must be achieved; then determine the values needed to express it.

Guidance: need → technical parameter

If the brief calls for operation from small airfields, ask: what take-off / landing distance is needed, under what conditions, and how will its value be justified? For a structural modification, ask: what function must the assembly retain, what loads does it transmit, and what geometric and installation constraints apply?

Technical documentation

This register belongs in the preamble of your design report. Include applicable certification documents, standards, technical specifications and maintenance or repair documentation. List textbooks and research papers in the references at the end of the report. Add to this register in subsequent modules.

Selected EASA Certification Specifications

Select documents relevant to your design object. Adding a document does not establish its applicability: check its scope, the appropriate issue and the relevant clauses. For an existing aircraft, check its certification basis. The latest issue is not automatically the applicable issue for every project.

CS-STAN applies only to the standard changes and repairs it defines and under its stated conditions. Determine the applicable regulatory basis separately for unmanned aircraft and space systems.

3. Derive technical requirements from needs

Core task

For each requirement, state its origin, measurable criterion, justification and verification method. You may start with preliminary values, but clearly indicate their status.

Questions to ask yourself: What must the structure / aircraft achieve? Which parameter describes it? Why was that value chosen? What conditions apply? How will compliance be verified? Have I stated a requirement or selected a solution prematurely?
Which requirement categories should you consider?

Depending on your brief, consider function, flight performance, mass, structural strength and stiffness, operation, safety / certification, manufacturing and cost. Not every category is relevant to every project. Do not select regulatory requirements solely by comparison with similar aircraft; establish applicability for your specific project.

How do origin, justification and verification differ?

Origin: the brief, a derived need or an applicable standard. Justification: why the criterion and value were chosen (literature, comparison, calculation or technical document). Verification: how compliance will later be demonstrated (calculation, analysis, test or drawing review).

4. Examine relationships and design trade-offs

Iterative design
Why may requirements need refinement?

Some targets are preliminary at the start of design and may be refined after mass, aerodynamic, structural or other calculations. Mandatory requirements in the brief and applicable regulatory requirements cannot be changed arbitrarily because they are difficult to meet.
