2. Further verification and sample expansion. Verify BETA ALIA-250 and Wisk Cora. Add AutoFlight Prosperity Gen 4, a documented winged passenger eVTOL prototype with a manufacturer-reported flight test. VX4 and Prosperity require variant-specific sources for tail classification. Classify layouts with two separate vertical stabilisers joined by a horizontal surface separately from V-tails.

3. Conclusion. V-tails are confirmed in 4 of 7 programmes (57.1%), with 5 of 7 classified (71.4%). Even if the two unclassified aircraft shared the same alternative layout, that layout could account for at most three cases, including the Cora. V-tails therefore have an absolute majority in this sample. If both unclassified aircraft had V-tails, the share would rise to 6/7 (85.7%). A wider conclusion requires a predefined programme list and consistent inclusion criteria. The observed share supports investigating V-tails further. 3. Normalise the geometry

Notation. S is wing area, b is wingspan, c̄ is mean aerodynamic chord, and l is the distance from the chosen datum to the tail aerodynamic centre. Use a consistent datum. This example uses the centre of gravity for the specified flight condition. SH and SV are horizontal and vertical tail areas. For an H-tail, sum both vertical stabiliser areas if their moment arms are identical. If the arms differ, account for the surfaces separately. ST is the total area of both V-tail panels measured in their planes. Γ is the angle of one panel from horizontal.

Comparison metrics. Wing aspect ratio AR = b²/S. Wing loading W/S = mg/S, with g = 9.80665 m/s². Compare P/m using the same type of power rating and operating condition. The conventional tail volume coefficients are VH = SHlH/(Sc̄) and VV = SVlV/(Sb).

For a symmetric V-tail with a common moment arm, use the geometric estimates SH,eq = STcos²Γ and SV,eq = STsin²Γ. The two trigonometric factors arise from projecting the flow angle change and the surface force. Downwash, local dynamic pressure, finite aspect ratio and surface interference require separate evaluation.

Use selected references with adequate geometry and justified sources. The button loads three labelled teaching rows to demonstrate the method.

4. Estimate preliminary V-tail geometry

Enter the wing geometry, tail moment arm and target VH and VV. Selecting a reference with both coefficients transfers them to the form. Adjust them as needed and justify your choice. The starting values illustrate the method.

Worked example

For conventional tail M-01, S = 18 m², b = 12 m, c̄ = 1.6 m, SH = 3.60 m², lH = 4.80 m, SV = 1.35 m² and lV = 4.65 m. This gives VH = 3.60 × 4.80 / (18 × 1.60) = 0.600 and VV = 1.35 × 4.65 / (18 × 12) = 0.02906.

With the same wing geometry and a 4.50 m V-tail moment arm, SH,eq = 3.84 m², SV,eq ≈ 1.395 m², ST ≈ 5.235 m² and Γ ≈ 31.1°. Each panel area is about 2.618 m². At a 4.00 m arm the total area rises to about 5.889 m² at the same angle. Use documented reference aircraft to justify your own targets.

Calculation sequence
  1. SH,eq = VHSc̄/l.
  2. SV,eq = VVSb/l.
  3. ST = SH,eq + SV,eq. Each panel has area ST/2.
  4. Γ = arctan(√(SV,eq/SH,eq)). Evaluate at least two values of l.

Wing · Aspect ratio and loading

Aircraft A and B both have a mass of 600 kg. Their wing areas are 12 and 15 m² and their wingspans 10 and 12 m, respectively. Their aspect ratios are 10²/12 = 8.33 and 12²/15 = 9.60. Wing loadings are 490.3 and 392.3 N/m², so B is 20% lower. At 650 kg and a target of 420 N/m², S = 650 × 9.80665 / 420 ≈ 15.18 m². Check mission speeds, maximum lift coefficient and structural mass.

Powerplant · Power and mass

A has 80 kW at 650 kg and B has 95 kW at 850 kg. For comparable maximum continuous ratings, P/m is 0.123 and 0.112 kW/kg. C has only a short-duration rating of 110 kW and is excluded from this comparison. At 750 kg, a target of 0.12 kW/kg gives 90 kW. Check power demand by mission phase and thermal limits.

Landing gear · Track and centre-of-gravity height

Compare main-wheel track B with centre-of-gravity height h above the supporting surface at the specified loading condition. For A, 1.8/0.9 gives B/h = 2.00. For B, 2.1/1.2 gives B/h = 1.75. With a preliminary target of 1.9 and h = 1.1 m, the project needs a track of 2.09 m. Assess overturning using the support polygon, the centre-of-gravity projection and the applied loads.
